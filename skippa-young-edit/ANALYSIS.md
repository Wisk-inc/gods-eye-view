Measured from the downloaded file: **1920×1080, 30 fps, 658 frames (21.93 s)**, H.264 ~25 Mbit/s, AAC stereo.
Frames 0–616 carry picture; frames 617–657 (1.4 s) are black.

The on-screen words are the chorus of the song it is cut to. They are not reproduced here: shots are numbered
**W01–W29** with letter counts, which is all the layout depends on.

## 1. What kind of edit this is

A **one-word-at-a-time 3D lyric edit**. Every word is a real extruded 3D object in its own small set; a virtual
camera whips from word to word. Around each word sits a kit of flat "shape layer" elements floating in depth
(tapered white strokes, rounded cards, bokeh rings, a spiky sunburst, dotted light strings, lens flares), and the
finished frame is pushed through a heavy finishing stack (glow, motion blur, flashes, iris wipes, polarity
inversion, letterbox bars, grain). It is the After Effects + Element 3D / C4D school of typography edit.

Structure: **≈17.3 s of lyric typography in ten looks → 3.1 s creator outro → 1.4 s black.**

## 2. Music, beats and where the hits land

- Tempo: **114.0 BPM** (the snare/hat band ticks every 0.263 s = one 8th note). Quarter note = 0.526 s =
  **15.79 frames**; 8th = 7.9 frames; 16th = 3.95 frames.
- Beat 0 sits at **0.116 s (frame 3.5)**. Beat `b` is at frame `3.48 + 15.789·b`.
- Rule of the edit: **words follow the vocal** (each word enters on the 16th note where it is sung);
  **flashes and scene transitions sit on quarter beats or 8ths**; **letters inside a word arrive 1–2 frames
  apart**, so a 5–7 letter word assembles inside one 16th–8th note.
- Transitions are *centred* on the beat: the smear/flash starts 3–5 frames early, and the new scene is
  readable on the beat.

## 3. Typography

- **Typeface feel:** very heavy, condensed, squared display caps, roughly **1 : 2** (width : height) per
  letter. Bowls are rectangles with chamfered corners; counters are **narrow vertical slots** about a third of
  the letter width; stems and counter are near-equal thirds. Terminals carry **small spurs**. Every edge is
  **rough-cut**: slight wobble (edges not quite parallel), fine tooth, and scattered chips. Letters differ
  slightly in height, baseline and rotation — it looks hand-cut.
- **Extrusion:** deep — 30–60 % of cap height — with a thin bright bevel. Words are always turned a little in
  3D (15–25° yaw, 10–20° pitch) so the sides show, usually toward the lower left.
- **Materials by look:** salmon-to-white faces with white bevel edges and saturated red sides (A–C, E–F);
  dark crimson faces (W08-09); gold with glowing yellow rims (D); silver/steel chrome with dark sides (G, I, J).
  Faces carry a soft top-to-bottom gradient and faint surface wear.
- **Scale:** cap height is normally **22–45 % of frame height**. Small exceptions: the long word W12 (~12 %),
  W13 (~14 %), the first appearance of the wide line (~9 %). Giant exception: W14 (~70 %, overflowing).
- **Tilt in frame:** almost never level. Typical roll −15° to +8°.
- **Entrances** (per letter, 1–2 frames apart, with overshoot): drop from above; rise from below; alternate
  drop/rise; fly in tumbling from a corner; slam from the lens (starts enormous); swing in from lower right.
- **Exits:** whip away with motion blur; tip backward (top receding) and darken; roll with the camera; defocus;
  get covered by a flash or iris.
- **Word morphs:** W05→W06 swap inside a punch zoom; W11→W12 and W26→W27 *extend* (the longer word begins
  with the letters of the shorter one, extra letters drop in).

## 7. What the recreation keeps and what it replaces

Kept: resolution, frame rate, every frame range above, the beat logic, the look of each section, the full
effect stack, layout and scale of every word and major shape.

Replaced with originals: the words (new text; yours via `lyrics.txt`), the typeface (an open-licence font re-cut
to match the lettering), the creator's logo and handle (an original roundel and a credit line), the planet (a
procedural texture), and the silhouette (an original falling figure). No footage, artwork or audio from the
reference is used.
